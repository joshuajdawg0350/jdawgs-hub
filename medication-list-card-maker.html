What it does

  • Label-exact entry: each medicine typed exactly as printed on its label — name, strength, amount as written, when taken, what for, prescriber, pharmacy.
  • Allergies up top: a prominent allergies box, plus emergency contact, doctors, and pharmacy on the same page.
  • Dated “last checked”: you confirm the date you checked the list against your bottles; any later change visibly flags the list as changed since the last check until you re-confirm — so anyone reading it knows how current it is.
  • Two printable outputs: one clear large-print list page, and a wallet-card layout at true card size (3.5in × 2.2in) that says how many more medicines are on the full list instead of silently leaving any off.
  • Stopped-medicines section: stopped medicines move to their own labeled section with the date — never silently deleted — so an appointment or hospital change is never lost.
  • Export always free: the printable list and card, plus a full CSV of the list.
  • Try it first: a labeled SAMPLE list runs the whole flow and removes cleanly without touching your real list.

Honest limits — read these first

  • An organizer, not medical advice. It never checks interactions, judges a dose, recommends a change, or gives medical advice — stated on the app’s first screen.
  • No dose alarms or reminders. A web app cannot fire reliable dose alarms, and this app does not claim to remind you to take anything.
  • The list is only as current as your last check. Update it the same day anything starts, stops, or changes, and bring it to every appointment, pharmacy pickup, and hospital stay.
  • Records are only what you type. Nothing is uploaded; there is no account; everything stays on your device. In an emergency call 911 (US) — the card is information for responders, not a substitute for care.
  • Disclosed-not-verified in our build audit: actual print rendering (the audit sandbox blocks the print dialog; the print layouts mirror the verified on-screen previews at fixed sizes), the CSV file’s downloaded bytes, and the Restore / permanent-remove clicks (buttons render; the shared handler was exercised through the move-to-stopped path).
