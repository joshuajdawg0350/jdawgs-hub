How to use it on a real call

1. The caller says who they are and what's happening. You say: "Hang on — Voiceproof me."  2. Both of you open CallCode with the same secret. 3. One of you reads the 6 digits and the word; the other checks their screen. Match = the person holding the secret is on the line. No match, an old code, or "I can't do it right now" during a money emergency = hang up and call them back on the number you already have.

What it is: the shared secret plus the current 5-minute window go through HMAC-SHA-256 (the same class of math behind authenticator apps) right in your browser. The first bytes of that hash become the digits and the word. A scammer can clone a voice from seconds of audio; they cannot clone a secret that was never spoken on a recorded line.

Honest limits: both phones need roughly correct clocks (phones set their clocks automatically — fine). Anyone who learns the secret can pass, so treat it like a house key: if it leaks, agree on a new phrase. CallCode proves the caller holds the circle's secret; it does not prove they aren't being forced — that's what the duress word in the CodeWord Forge is for.

Privacy: this page makes no network calls with your secret. The math runs on your device; if you tick "remember," the phrase is stored only in this browser on this device.
