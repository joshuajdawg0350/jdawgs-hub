Your camera roll is full of near-duplicates, blurry shots, and screenshots — and the cleaner apps want $5.95–$11.99 a week to deal with it. What it does

  • Near-duplicate groups: photos that look alike are grouped side by side at readable size, with file size and date under each. The largest/sharpest is marked as a suggested keeper — a suggestion only, never an auto-decision.
  • Blur scores with the numbers shown: every photo gets a sharpness score and a plain label (Sharp / Soft / Blurry). The number is right there — no black-box "AI" verdict.
  • Screenshot & biggest-file lanes: screenshots grouped by month, and your largest files listed with sizes — the two fastest honest wins in any camera roll.
  • Your decisions, exportable: mark Keep or Delete-candidate, undo any mark, and export a plain list (name, size, decision) plus the total estimated space. You delete in your own gallery app, from the list — nothing is ever deleted for you.
  • Try it in 30 seconds: a built-in SAMPLE set, clearly labeled, runs the whole flow — grouping, blur labels, decisions, export — with none of your photos.

Honest limits — read these first

  • It cannot scan your whole gallery. No web app can. You pick the photos it may look at, in batches — that picker is also your privacy guarantee: unpicked photos are never touched.
  • It cannot delete photos from your phone. No web app can do that either. This app helps you decide and gives you the list; deleting happens in your own gallery app, under your control.
  • Similarity and blur scores are heuristics — good guides with the numbers shown, not proof. The final call on every photo is yours.
  • Estimated space is an estimate until the files are actually deleted in your gallery.
  • Disclosed-not-verified in our build audit: the native file picker on a real phone (the app uses the standard file dialog, which our test rig cannot operate — the identical analysis path is proven end-to-end by the SAMPLE set), the downloaded list landing in a real phone's Downloads folder, and the storage-blocked fallback branch (storage is wrapped so a restricted browser runs session-only with a plain notice; verified by code inspection).
