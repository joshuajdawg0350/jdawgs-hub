What it does

  • Pick the job: a Wi-Fi card for guests or customers, a contact card (vCard), a link card, a payment-link card (the code carries the link you paste — nothing more), or a plain-text card.
  • Made on your phone: a real QR encoder runs inside the app — nothing is uploaded, no account, nothing is stored after the session.
  • Proven before you print: the app decodes its own code back and shows the exact text a scanner will read. Export stays locked until the read-back matches what you typed, byte for byte. Too-long input is refused with the exact count — never silently cut off.
  • A card worth printing: your title and one plain line on a neat card; export the card as a PNG, the code alone as a PNG, or print it. A minimum print-size guide is stated in the app.
  • Try it first: a labeled SAMPLE Wi-Fi card runs the whole flow — make, prove, export — with a printed fake password.

Honest limits — read these first

  • Static means never expires — and never editable. The code holds your data directly, so no subscription can switch it off. The trade-off is real: if the Wi-Fi password or link changes, you print a new card.
  • A payment-link card only opens the link you pasted. This app does not process payments, does not check that the link is yours, and does not guarantee where it leads.
  • Anyone who can see a Wi-Fi card can scan it — and scanning it reveals the password. That is what a Wi-Fi QR code is; the app says so before you make one.
  • Proof covers our rendering, not your printer. The decode-back check proves the code on screen is correct; a tiny, blurry, or damaged printout can still fail a scanner — print the code at least 2.5 cm (1 inch) wide.
  • Disclosed-not-verified in our build audit: a physical phone camera scanning a printed card, and the printout itself (the app's print view shows only the card; your browser's own Print option does the printing). The encoder and decoder were verified byte-exact against independent decoders in the lab.
