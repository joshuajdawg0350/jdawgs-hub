J-Dawgs Home Care Log™

A house runs on a schedule nobody writes down — filters, flushes, gutters, detector batteries. Home Care Log writes it down: your systems and their details, what's due next, and a service record that proves what was done. $4.99 once.

What it does

  • Systems list with the details that get lost: HVAC, water heater, roof/gutters, detectors, appliances — brand, model, serial, install year, and the filter size or battery type you always end up re-measuring. Optional nameplate photo stays on your device.
  • Due-next home screen: every upkeep task sorted by due date, overdue flagged in plain words.
  • One-tap done, done right: marking a task done writes the service-log entry (date, who did it, cost, note) and rolls the next due date forward from the day you actually did it — not from the old due date.
  • Editable starter schedule: filter change, water-heater flush, gutter clean, detector test/batteries, furnace/AC service, dryer-vent clean — plain starting-point intervals you can change.
  • Reminders that are honest: a .ics calendar file per task with 7-day and 1-day alarms. No fake push notifications.
  • Handover report, always free: a printable home report (for a buyer, insurer, or the next owner) plus a CSV of the full service history — never gated.
  • Try it first: a labeled SAMPLE house runs the full schedule-to-report flow and removes cleanly without touching your real records.

Honest limits — read these first

  • Intervals are common-practice starting points — not manufacturer instructions, building code, or safety certification. The manual for your own equipment wins.
  • Doing a task on schedule does not guarantee a system won't fail. The app keeps records; it does not inspect anything.
  • Reminders are calendar files (.ics), not a notification service.
  • Records are only what you type; photos stay on the device.
  • Warranty Vault is about who pays if something breaks. Home Care Log is about what to do on schedule — and keeping the record of what was done.
  • Disclosed-not-verified in our build audit: the downloaded .ics file's bytes on a real phone (its generator and due dates were verified in-app) and the nameplate photo picker on a real phone.
