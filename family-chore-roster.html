What it does

  • People as plain names: household members are just names — no accounts, no invites, no emails.
  • Chores with plain intervals: daily, every 2 days, every 3 days, weekly, or a custom number of days — each chore goes to a fixed person or rotates through people in the order you set.
  • A rotation you can watch advance: tap Done and the chore moves to the next person, with the next due date rolled forward from the day it was actually done — the new person and new date are shown, never changed silently.
  • This-week board: everything due in the next 7 days plus anything overdue, sorted by day, overdue flagged in plain words.
  • Printable fridge chart: the week as one clean page that prints by itself — the chart on the fridge is how the household shares it.
  • Calendar export: a locally generated .ics file for anyone who wants chores on a calendar.
  • Done history + SAMPLE household: a log of what was done, by whom, and when — and a labeled SAMPLE household that runs the whole flow and removes cleanly without touching your real roster.

Honest limits — read these first

  • One device only — it does NOT sync. The roster lives on one phone or computer. There are no accounts or invites. The printable fridge chart and the .ics calendar file are the sharing mechanisms, stated plainly.
  • Free options exist, honestly: if a static printable grid is all you need, free chart makers do that, and a free cloud app (Donetick) shares a chart between phones. This roster is for the rotation engine, the board, and a file you own with no ads.
  • A roster records the plan and the done-taps; it cannot make anyone do a chore, and it never claims otherwise. Records are only what you type; nothing is uploaded.
  • Disclosed-not-verified in our build audit: actual printing (the audit sandbox blocks the print dialog; the app points you to your browser’s own Print option and the print layout isolates the chart) and the downloaded .ics file landing on a real phone.
