J-Dawgs On-Device AI Writing Kit™

Your browser may already carry a local AI model. This kit finds out — it checks which built-in AI features your browser actually offers, shows you the answer in plain words, and then summarizes and translates on your device. Your text is never sent anywhere: there is no cloud fallback in this app, at all.

What it does

  • Capability card first: on load, the app actually detects which built-in AI APIs your browser exposes (Summarizer, Translator, Language Detector, the Prompt model, Writer/Rewriter/Proofreader) and tells you — available here, or not available on this browser, with the reason in plain words — before any button lights up.
  • Summarize & translate on-device: where the real local APIs are detected, they do the work, and every result is labeled with the engine that made it: "Made on your device by [API name]".
  • Mechanical checks on any browser: word / sentence / paragraph counts, reading time, repeated words, double spaces, very-long-sentence flags, and a readability score — plain JavaScript, working on phones too, and every one labeled "Mechanical check — not AI".
  • No fake AI, ever: where an AI feature isn't available on your browser, its button says so and stays off. Nothing is silently sent to a server to make it look like it worked.
  • Try it in 30 seconds: a built-in SAMPLE text, clearly labeled, exercises every check without pasting anything private.

Honest limits — read these first

  • AI features need a browser that offers a local model — today that means desktop Chrome on capable hardware. They do not work in most phone browsers yet, and the app tells you plainly which case you are in. On a phone you still get the full mechanical kit.
  • No cloud fallback exists. If the local model is unavailable, the AI buttons say so. Your text is never uploaded to make them work — the audit proved zero external requests.
  • The local model is not a frontier cloud model: shorter context, fewer languages. That is the trade for private, offline, no-account — stated here, not buried.
  • Rewrite/Proofreader browser APIs are still in trials (checked Nov 2026) — where your browser doesn't offer them, those buttons show the reason instead of faking output.
  • Disclosed-not-verified in our build audit: real local-model output on capable desktop Chrome hardware can't be exercised in our test sandbox — the translation path was driven to the real API and labeled correctly, but the browser's own language-pack download is blocked there. The summarize/rewrite handlers follow the same detected-API pattern and were source-inspected.
