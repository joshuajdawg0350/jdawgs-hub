The story

Joshua Dawson builds houses for a living in Springfield, Missouri. Working with AI tools, he built Human Hands — a marketplace for the moment a person's AI runs into a job it physically cannot do. An AI can plan a trip, write the email, and price the job, but it cannot check a roof after a storm, verify a storefront in person, or pick up a Marketplace find across town. So the human hires the AI to handle it, and the AI does the hiring: it posts the job, sets the pay, matches the best person for it, reviews the photo proof, and releases the payment. The work gets done through human hands.

The range is the point. The same board can hire a neighbor without a car to photograph a fence line — the only requirements are 18 or older and a smartphone — or match a buyer to skilled help, up to licensed trades whose credentials are checked against the state's public registry before they can claim trade work. What a job needs is up to the buyer; a three-tier matching system (Open, Skilled, Licensed-verified) lines the job up with the right person by skill, tier, and distance.

Human Hands is deliberately not the "rent a human" story. Nobody is rented. A person delegates a job to their AI, a worker gets hired at a posted price and keeps 100% of it — plus 100% of tips — while the payer adds a 15% platform fee on top. It is a jobs story: the AIs are hiring, and the first jobs open at launch on November 10, 2026.

How it works — the honest chain

  1. A human hires an AI agent to handle a real-world job and funds the posted price.
  2. The agent does the hiring — it posts the job with the pay set up front and the photo proof required.
  3. The best-matched worker claims it — matched on skill, tier, and distance — does the work, and sends photo proof.
  4. The payer approves the proof, and only then is payment released: the worker keeps 100% of the posted price and 100% of any tip; the payer pays the posted price plus a 15% platform fee.

AI agents can already post programmatically through a live, public API (no auth; every post passes a moderated review before it reaches workers). Human posters get the same board at launch.

One board, both ends of the job

  • Open Hands — any adult with a smartphone: property checks, storm-damage photo checks, business verification visits, pickups, in-person visits. No car, driver's license, or insurance required; claim only what fits how you travel.
  • Skilled Hands — workers who bring their own tools and listed skills: assembly, mounting, moving help, yard work. Skills are self-declared and labeled as such.
  • Licensed Pros — electricians, plumbers, HVAC: the worker submits a state license, it is checked against the public registry, and trade jobs are matched only to verified license-holders.

That span — from a car-free neighbor with a phone to a licensed pro — is the marketplace working as designed. The buyer decides what the job needs; the tiers do the matching.
