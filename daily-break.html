What it does

  • A new board every day: the same board for everyone that day, derived offline from the date — no server, no account. One straightforward group, two medium, one stretch — never a trick.
  • Boards checked for fairness: every board in the first 90 seeded days plus the full practice bank was hand-verified at audit — exactly one valid solution, no full false group of four. Themes are specific, everyday, and checkable; no hidden-letter or sound tricks.
  • Think before you commit: tap tiles into a sort tray, rearrange them freely, and only then tap Submit. A wrong submit tells you plainly how many belong together — "Three of these belong together", "Two of these belong together", or "None of these four go together" — exactly as the rules promise.
  • Last 7 days + practice: yesterday's boards stay open for a week and a practice bank is always there, so the habit does not end with one puzzle a day. Your streak and results live on your phone only.
  • A result line you can share: date, solved or failed, mistakes used, groups in the order you found them — plain text you copy yourself. No leaderboard, no account, no tracking.
  • Try it first: a labeled SAMPLE board runs the full game before you commit, with your streak untouched.

Honest limits — read these first

  • Free versions exist: free grouping puzzles live on the web, including the famous newspaper one — this is ours: original boards checked for fairness, in a file you own, with no ads and no subscription. The newspaper's daily is free too; its archive and Bot analysis sit behind a subscription ($4.99–$5.99/mo), and that is the paywall this app replaces with a one-time price.
  • No leaderboard: there is no way to compare inside the app beyond copying your result line — it is a file, not a service.
  • Boards older than 7 days leave the daily list; practice always has boards to play.
  • Disclosed, not verified: the Copy button's clipboard write could not be observed in the audit sandbox (the result text itself was verified on screen; the handler falls back to select-and-copy where clipboard access is blocked).
