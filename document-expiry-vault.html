What it does

  • Per-person documents: holder, type (passport, driver’s license, visa, national ID, certification, insurance policy, or your own), expiry date, and a renewal lead time you set per document.
  • Staged early, in plain words: one family list sorted by expiry, each document flagged — expired, renewal window open, on track — with staging at 12, 6, and 3 months driven by your lead times. Expired documents stay visible, labeled expired.
  • Your own renewal checklist per document: what to bring, where to apply, what it cost last time — in your words, never scraped official fees or procedures.
  • Calendar reminders: .ics files at your lead times per document, generated locally.
  • Exports always free: a printable renewal sheet and CSV. A labeled SAMPLE family runs the whole flow without touching your real documents.
  • Dates only, by design: the form records expiry date, type, holder, and a short reference note — it never offers a field for a full document number, scan, or PIN.

Honest limits — read these first

  • It records the dates you type. It does not verify any document with any authority, does not renew or file anything, and does not guarantee entry to any country.
  • Lead times are yours, not legal advice. The 6-months-validity rule is general information, not a promise for any destination.
  • Reminders are calendar files, not a notification service. Records live on your device only; nothing is uploaded; it works offline.
  • Free/other options exist, honestly: KeepValid is currently completely free — as a cloud service under an account that stores your documents on their servers. Almanac’s Vault is $4.99 once with cloud sync and country renewal guides we don’t offer. This vault is $3.99 once and is for households who want no cloud at all.
  • Disclosed-not-verified in our build audit: the downloaded CSV/.ics file bytes landing on a real phone (the audit sandbox cannot inspect downloads; the generation code was verified in source and the export controls are wired to it).
