What it does

  • Spend-down ledger: add a card once (store, kind, starting balance, expiry), then log each spend — the remaining balance recomputes in exact cents, with edit and undo that restore the exact prior balance. An over-spend is refused in plain words, never recorded.
  • Use-first home: active cards sorted by soonest expiry first, expired cards flagged in plain words, and a total-held headline that sums what you actually still hold.
  • Last-4 only, by design: the form never offers a full card number or PIN field — a reference note is all it stores. Nothing to leak because it was never collected.
  • Calendar reminders: one .ics file per expiring card with 30-day and 7-day reminders, generated locally.
  • Exports always free: CSV (every card + every spend) and a printable wallet list. A card spent to exactly $0.00 moves to a used-up section — never silently deleted.
  • Labeled SAMPLE wallet: three sample cards run the whole flow and remove cleanly without touching your real cards.

Honest limits — read these first

  • It cannot check a live balance — no account, no email access, no retailer connection, ever. Balances are what you type and spend down. Services that check real balances need your email or card numbers; this one refuses both.
  • Expiry wording is general information: US store gift-card funds cannot expire before 5 years under federal law; promotional cards and store credit can expire sooner. The app shows the date and the kind you recorded.
  • Reminders are calendar files, not a notification service. Records live on your device only; nothing is uploaded; it works offline.
  • Free/other options exist, honestly: Woolly is completely free on iPhone/iPad (it stores full card numbers and PINs and shows no export — a different trade-off), and CardWarden Premium Offline is $9.99 one time with barcode storage we don’t offer. This wallet is $4.99 once, runs on any phone’s browser, and never holds a full number.
  • Disclosed-not-verified in our build audit: the downloaded CSV/.ics file bytes landing on a real phone (the audit sandbox cannot inspect downloads; the generation code was verified in source and the export controls are wired to it).
