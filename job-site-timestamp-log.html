What it does

  • Pick photos you already took: the standard file picker, several at once. Nothing is uploaded; your originals are never altered — each one is fingerprinted before stamping and re-checked after, so you can see it did not change.
  • Stamp a copy: a burned-in strip on a copy of the photo shows the date and time — from the photo's own EXIF capture time when it has one, otherwise the time you stamped it. The strip says which source was used, for every photo.
  • A real job log: photos collect under a job name, sorted by capture time, with a note per photo and a location only if the photo already carries one or you type it — never tracked.
  • Export locally: a printable report (thumbnails, stamps, notes, dates) generated on your phone, plus each stamped copy as its own download.
  • Try it first: a built-in SAMPLE job runs the whole flow — pick, stamp, log, report — with no real photos.

Honest limits — read these first

  • A stamp is documentation, not proof. It shows what the clock said. Device clocks can be changed and EXIF can be edited — this app never claims a stamped photo is tamper-proof, certified, or court-proof, and you should not trust any app that does.
  • It stamps copies of photos you pick. A web app cannot stamp photos at capture inside your camera app — the free stamp cameras do that half; this app does the half they refuse: the photos already on your phone.
  • No EXIF time, no invented time. If a photo carries no capture time, the stamp uses the moment you stamped it and says so.
  • Your files never leave your device. No upload, no account.
  • Disclosed-not-verified in our build audit: the native photo picker on a real phone and downloads landing in a phone's file system (standard browser controls, exercised in code; provable only on a real device). Our EXIF accuracy was verified against constructed test photos with known capture times — parsed to the exact second.
