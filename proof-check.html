What it is

Proof Check is a web app you open on your phone — no app-store download, no account, no upload. Modern cameras and AI tools are starting to sign the photos they produce with Content Credentials: a tamper-evident record inside the file saying what made it, when, and whether AI was involved. Almost nobody outside the industry can read that record. Proof Check reads it for you, in plain words, entirely on your device.

You get one of exactly three honest verdicts — never a made-up percentage: Signed and consistent · Declares AI involvement · No provenance found — which proves nothing either way.

When you buy it, you also get it as a single HTML file you can keep and run yourself. One payment of $4.99. Yours forever.

What it does

  • Provenance card, verdict first — the answer in plain English, with the evidence underneath: Content Credentials present or not, who signed the record (camera or AI tool), when, and what it declares
  • IPTC source type, translated — the file's own declared source (for example, a file that declares itself AI-generated reads exactly that way on the card)
  • EXIF basics — device, timestamp, and whether GPS data is present (yes/no — never a map, never coordinates you didn't ask for)
  • Batch mode for sellers — check a whole folder of listing photos in one pass, the way a marketplace seller or a photo-proof service actually works
  • Built-in samples — three clearly labeled sample files exercise every verdict, so you can see exactly what each card looks like before trusting it with a real photo
  • Private by design — every byte is parsed on your device; the photo never leaves your phone; works fully offline after it loads

The honest limits — printed here and in the app

  • A missing credential proves nothing. Metadata strips easily — screenshots and platform recompression remove it. "No provenance found" is a statement about the file, not about the photo's honesty.
  • Proof Check reads records, it does not guess. It gives no "AI probability" score — detector apps that do are guessing at pixels, and we won't sell a guess as a finding.
  • A signed credential proves the origin claims in the record — not that the scene photographed was real.
  • The signature is reported as "present and well-formed." Full cryptographic trust-list verification is not performed, and the card says so plainly.
  • SynthID pixel watermarks cannot be read by this app.
  • Disclosed-not-verified in our build audit: picking a real file through the phone's file picker and tapping a batch row (the audit sandbox cannot inject real files — both use the exact parser path validated against constructed test files), and the dark-mode palette, which follows the same design system but was not screenshot-verified in the audit browser.
